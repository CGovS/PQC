      PQC means post-quantum cryptography: algorithms designed to resist known quantum attacks. It runs on ordinary computers.

      01

      Find where encryption lives

      This app sends every vault operation through one shared interface. Real companies first inventory their apps, certificates, vendors, and stored data.

      02

      Choose a new way to protect the key

      RSA becomes ML-KEM. ML-KEM establishes a shared secret; HKDF derives an AES key; AES-GCM encrypts and authenticates the flag.

      03

      Test before you switch

      Verify owner access, tampering, and wrong keys. Company rollouts also need compatible protocols, key rotation, data migration, and downgrade protection.

      What this experiment proves—and what it doesn’t +

      RSA: the browser recovers the key from public data with a Fourier-based simulation of Shor’s period-finding, then decrypts the ciphertext. A SHA-256 digest checks the recovered bytes. Tiny textbook RSA is also easy to break classically; this demonstrates a mechanism, not a practical RSA-2048 break.

      PQC: ML-KEM key generation, encapsulation, decapsulation, and AES-GCM encryption run on the local Python server. Owner access, wrong-key rejection, and tamper rejection are tested each run. The Shor dispatcher reports an unsupported target; it does not measure a “flat PQC spectrum.”

      Simulation: JavaScript and NumPy simulate the ideal measurement distribution, not physical hardware or a Qiskit gate circuit. The animated maze is an illustration, not the algorithm’s search space. Random measurements can repeat.

      Deployment: kyber-py is an educational, non-constant-time implementation. This lab is not production cryptography or a certified module. Swapping a setting here does not automatically migrate old ciphertext, certificates, or an enterprise.
